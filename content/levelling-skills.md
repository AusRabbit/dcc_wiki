On the tutorial floors (Floors 1 and 2), you get skills by trying things. There are fifty or
sixty skills in the game and you won't get them all, so this is where you decide what sort
of crawler you are. Later on, skills mostly come from magic, potions and gear.

## During play

Any time you attempt a skill, it gets ticked in the **Used** column on your sheet — even if
you're untrained (rank 0). Untrained attempts roll with Disadvantage. The GM may also tick
skills you used in the story without naming them.

## Back at the safe room

- **Roll to level.** For each ticked skill below rank 5, roll a d20. Equal to or above its current rank and it goes up one rank. Below, nothing happens.
- **Keep one new skill.** Choose one of the untrained skills you ticked. It becomes rank 1.
- **The rest fall away.** All the other rank 0 skills are cleared and the ticks reset.

Try ten new things and you still only keep one, so mix it up: use the skills you have so they
rank up, and try ones you don't have so you've got a choice to make.

On the dashboard, the dice button in the **Used** column rolls the advancement, and the blue
**Keep this** button picks the skill you keep. Leave the rest.

## Grinding

Grinding means spending hours killing the easiest mobs you can find. It's played as a
montage: no individual rolls, no looting, and no risk during a normal grind. You can do it
on the way somewhere — back to a safe room, for example.

- **Hours** — five a day is the safe limit. A [[neighbourhood-map]] gives you credit for one extra hour, so five counts as six.
- **Spend them** — add hours to skills in the **Grinding** column. When a skill has hours equal to its current rank, its roll button pulses green. Roll a d20: equal to or above the rank and it goes up.
- **Spread them** — you can only grind each skill once a day, and spare hours can't go back into the same skill. Lots of low-rank skills get more out of an hour than one high one.
- **Levels** — grinding hours also count towards levelling up, slowly. Killing a neighbourhood boss is worth a level. On the tutorial floors you level up, but you can't spend attribute points yet.

The GM asks for a sentence or two about what happened during the grind.

The lesson from [[session-02]]: Darcy sank most of his hours into one high skill, failed the
roll, and had nothing to show for them. Frank spread his hours and did better.
